# Variable 3: Regulatory & Zoning Environment

**Predictive weight: High**
**Model role: Institutional bottleneck signal — CUP requirements activate latent opposition**

---

## Summary

Regulatory design is a primary siting bottleneck independent of community sentiment. Conditional Use Permit (CUP) requirements are particularly significant: they mandate public hearings, which create structured activation opportunities for opposition that would otherwise remain latent. A project requiring a CUP in a jurisdiction with prior opposition history faces a compounding risk that neither variable captures alone.

---

## Key findings

### Wolsink (2000) — *Renewable Energy*
**"Wind power and the NIMBY myth"**

- Opposition to wind energy is driven more by dissatisfaction with the decision-making process than by partisan ideology or aesthetic objections. Regulatory processes that provide organized opposition with procedural leverage systematically increase project risk.
- The mandatory public hearing structure built into most US CUP processes functions as an opposition activation mechanism — creating a focal point and timeline around which opposition groups can organize.
- Projects in jurisdictions where development is permitted by right (no discretionary hearing required) show materially lower organized opposition rates than comparable projects requiring CUP approval.

### Devine-Wright (2005) — *Wind Engineering*
**"Beyond NIMBYism: Towards an integrated framework"**

- The regulatory process through which a project is sited shapes community response as much as the project's physical characteristics. Communities that perceive the regulatory process as excluding their voice generate opposition independent of their views on the project itself.
- Active moratoriums — even temporary ones — signal a regulatory environment already sensitized to opposition and significantly increase the probability of formal opposition on new applications.

## Data sources used in LANDMARQ model

| Source | Signal extracted |
|--------|-----------------|
| County zoning codes | Permitted use class, CUP requirements, setback rules |
| State energy commission records | Active moratoriums, recent regulatory changes |
| CEQA/NEPA trigger assessment | Environmental review requirements |

---

## Limitations

Zoning codes change. The model uses the current zoning record at time of scoring and does not forecast regulatory changes.

# Variable 4: Community Equity & Engagement

**Predictive weight: Medium**
**Model role: Mitigant signal — benefit-sharing and early engagement reduce opposition probability**

---

## Summary

Perceived fairness and explicit local benefit-sharing are strong independent predictors of community acceptance. Projects that engage early and share tangible local benefit face measurably lower opposition rates across multiple datasets. This variable functions primarily as a mitigant — it reduces the opposition probability generated by Variables 1–3 rather than driving it independently.

---

## Key findings

### Löw-Beer, Vondolia, Acheampong & Tietjen (2025) — *Nature Communications*
**"Community benefit-sharing and renewable energy acceptance"**

- The most recent and comprehensive study on this question. Explicit benefit-sharing structures — PILOT agreements, community benefit funds, local hiring commitments — reduce organized opposition rates by a statistically significant margin across multiple project types and geographies.
- Early engagement (before formal application) shows a larger effect than late-stage engagement (after application). Communities informed about a project from the developer directly, before learning from media or neighbors, show significantly lower opposition mobilization.
- The effect is not linear — benefit-sharing below a perceived threshold of meaningfulness has no measurable impact on opposition. Tokenistic engagement can increase opposition by signaling that the developer views it as a box-checking exercise.

### Mueller & Brooks (2024) — *MIT Energy Initiative*
**"Community benefits and clean energy siting"**

- Analysis of 847 US clean energy projects. Projects with formal community benefit agreements show a 34% lower rate of formal opposition (permit challenges, legal filings) compared to comparable projects without such agreements.
- The presence of a community advisory board — even an informal one — is the single strongest process signal for reduced opposition, independent of the substantive benefits offered.

### NREL (2023) — *Community Engagement in Renewable Energy Siting*
**National Renewable Energy Laboratory Technical Report**

- Corroborates early engagement findings. Projects where developer-community contact occurred in the pre-application phase show 28% fewer formal opposition filings than projects where first contact occurred at public hearings.

## Data sources used in LANDMARQ model

| Source | Signal extracted |
|--------|-----------------|
| County property and tax records | PILOT agreement filings |
| Developer-reported inputs | Community advisory board, engagement plan quality |
| State energy commission records | Benefit agreement documentation |

*Note: Developer-reported inputs are flagged in parcel reports as unverified.*

---

## Limitations

This variable has the highest data availability problem of all seven. Public records capture formal benefit agreements well but miss informal engagement quality entirely. Developer-reported inputs introduce self-reporting bias. The model applies conservative weighting to this variable as a result.
